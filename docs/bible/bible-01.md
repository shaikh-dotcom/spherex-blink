# 01 - Brief

**What:** a 150 second film (4500 frames, 1920x1080, 30 fps) demonstrating the SPHEREx Blink Comparator web app, in the same code-driven Remotion style as the sibling project minilocker-film: the real app is rebuilt in code and a virtual camera flies around it, with captions and callouts on the beat.

**Challenge:** NASA Space Apps 2026, "Planet X and SPHEREx". The app helps people find moving objects in SPHEREx infrared images, tells a fixed star from a mover, validates itself against a known asteroid, and shows what a distant Planet X would do through parallax.

**Judging criteria (NASA Space Apps):** Influence, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation. Map:
- Relevance: S2 to S4 show the tool answering the challenge directly (real SPHEREx frames, blink, mover check).
- Validity: S5 (reality check against a known asteroid, 148 of 150) and S8 (limits, simulated vs real) are the credibility scenes. They must be exact and modest.
- Creativity: the blink comparator itself (the Pluto technique) and the parallax lab (S6).
- Influence: free, public, one link, runs in a browser, flags export as CSV or JSON (S7, S9).
- Presentation: calm pacing, one idea per beat, every number true.

**The one-sentence message:** "Real infrared images of one patch of sky. Find what moves."

**Story spine:** the sky stands still, except one dot (S0) -> the name (S1) -> real data (S2) -> blink it (S3) -> click the dot and ask "star or mover?" (S4) -> check the tool against a known asteroid (S5) -> what would Planet X do, and what does the tool say about a real mover (S6) -> flag and export (S7) -> what is real, simulated and limited (S8) -> one link (S9).

**Not in the film:** no claim of discovery; no voice-over (captions only); no screenshots or recordings of the app; no stock footage.

**Length note:** the Space Apps judging guide sets no video length. Check the length limit on YOUR submission page. `npm run cut30` (patch 05) renders a 30 second cut of the strongest beats if a short version is needed.
