# 10 - Honesty

Allowed: "real SPHEREx data", "Quick Release 2", "a sample of the sky", "prototype", "candidate for follow-up", "found the known asteroid in the pixels", "moves", "gone from the other frames", "nearby object: asteroid or comet", "simulated practice field".

Forbidden: "discovery", "discovered", "found Planet X", "detected a planet", "confirmed", "proves", "AI finds planets", any claim that the tool finds unknown objects, any invented statistic, any invented date or coordinate, any claim about SPHEREx beyond the app's own text.

Must show (S8): that the practice field is simulated; that each field holds only 2 to 6 of the six detectors; that the stricter check passes in 107 of 150 frames (not all); that Ceres passes 0 of 8 despite moving far, not yet explained (shown or said plainly if the limits scene lists the reality check); that no coverage is a hatch.

Planet X result: the measured pair in the film is the asteroid Pallas. The app's verdict is "Nearby object: asteroid or comet". The film shows that verdict. It does NOT show a made-up distant fit. It may explain what a "distant" verdict would say ("Consistent with a distant body ... a candidate, not a detection ... needs confirming in another pass") by showing the lab's simulated track at a chosen distance, always labelled "Planet X @ d AU (simulated)" exactly as the app labels it.

Simulated or illustrative graphics (the S0 star field, the parallax diagram) carry a small label saying so ("Illustration").

Attribution: S9 shows the IRSA acknowledgement text from evidence verbatim. Reference pictures and planet pictures shown anywhere must keep the credit the app shows.
