PRODUCT IN 6 LINES: The app flips (blinks) between SPHEREx infrared observations of the same patch of sky taken on different days; stars stay put and anything that moves jumps out, the technique Clyde Tombaugh used to find Pluto. It runs in the browser on real SPHEREx Quick Release 2 data (20 real fields, 219 frames, plus one simulated practice field). A mover check tells a fixed star from a mover; a reality check re-finds the known asteroid in the pixels (Pallas 13 of 13 frames, 0.57 px rms); a Planet X lab computes the exact parallax a distant body would show from Earth's motion and fits a distance to a measured mover; flags export as CSV or JSON. FILM FACTS
- 1920x1080, 30 fps, 4500 frames = 150 s. 120 BPM: beat = 15 f, bar = 60 f, 75 bars. Land every scene cut, reveal, big camera move and SFX hit on a beat.
- Scenes: S0 Hook 0-299 | S1 Title 300-599 | S2 Data 600-1079 | S3 Blink 1080-1679 | S4 Inspect 1680-2219 | S5 Check 2220-2699 | S6 PlanetX 2700-3419 | S7 Flag 3420-3779 | S8 Limits 3780-4199 | S9 Outro 4200-4499.
- The app UI is designed at a LOGICAL size of 1600x900, centred on the 1920x1080 stage. A virtual camera (translate + scale 1.0 to about 2.6) does all zooming so text stays vector-sharp. Scene transitions are camera moves or match-cuts, never crossfades or wipes.
- Signature ease cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1). Springs: UI pop {18,170,0.9}; panel {22,120,1}; camera {26,90,1.1}; settle {14,220,1}. Nothing just appears: anticipation, decisive move, soft settle. Linear motion only for the blink flicker, typing, particles.

ARCHITECTURE RULES (so parallel sessions fit together; the full contract is docs/bible/bible-04.md)
- The app is ONE persistent component, <FilmApp/>. Its whole visual state is FilmState (src/config/filmState.ts), a pure function of the global frame: useFilmState(frame) = DEFAULT_FILM_STATE merged with sceneState(frame).
- Each scene file exports FIVE things and nothing else is needed: SN_CAMERA (CameraKeyframe[]), SN_CURSOR (CursorWaypoint[]), SN_STATE (frame => FilmStatePatch), and the overlay component. Scenes NEVER re-implement app UI.
- Each UI component file owns its slice of FilmState: it exports XState, X_DEFAULT and the component. UI components are dumb: props in, pixels out; they never read the frame to decide story. An owner may ADD optional-looking fields to its own slice (give every new field a default) but must not rename or remove a field that already exists, because scene patches written in parallel already use them.
- FILE OWNERSHIP. Each patch lists the files it owns. Write ONLY those files. If you need a change in a file you do not own, do not make it: put it under "Requests" in your ledger block.
- Camera and focus names come only from src/config/focus.ts (FOCUS_NAMES). Scenes use kf(frame, focusName, opts) from src/config/camera.ts.

MOTION RULES (full text docs/guide/motion-rules.md)
- No shot is still: a hold over 60 frames gets a 3-5 percent camera push. Camera moves take 24+ frames; hold 10 frames between moves; pan speed under 160 px per frame. Zoom above 2.0 only on one small card for at most 60 frames. Text the viewer must read stays at 2.0 or lower.
- Every scene supplies camera keyframes for its WHOLE range, starts from the previous scene's last pose, and never leaves the camera on a cropped corner.
- NEVER use backdrop-filter (Chrome draws a mirrored copy under a 3D transform). Dim or blur the app with a plain overlay.
- Captions never sit on live UI text.
